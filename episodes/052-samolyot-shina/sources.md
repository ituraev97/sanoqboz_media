# 052 — Samolyot shinalari nega qo‘nishda yorilmaydi
- Jalopnik, 23 Feb 2024 (Ryan Erik King): Boeing 737 MAX 9 touches down at "over 130 mph" (≈209 km/h → "taxminan 200 km/soat"); mechanics inflate to "around 200 psi", multiples of a road-car tyre; Air Canada: main-gear tyres last 300–450 landings; Michelin: can retread an aircraft tyre up to seven times: https://jalopnik.com/why-airplane-tires-last-less-than-500-landings-1851282309
- The Points Guy, 10 May 2019 (Mike Arnot, 787 engineer): 787 main tyres "around 200 psi compared to 35 psi in your Corolla" (200/35 ≈ 5.7 → "olti baravar", own calc); filled with dry nitrogen because moisture could vaporise at landing temperatures; wheel accelerates "from nothing to over 1,000 rpm on touchdown almost immediately"; thermal relief plugs melt to prevent tyres bursting when hot brakes overheat: https://thepointsguy.com/airline/all-about-aircraft-tires
- Code 7700 (aircraft_tires): nitrogen "will not sustain combustion", reduces oxidation, lower moisture content: https://code7700.com/aircraft_tires.htm
- "Qo‘nishda deyarli turgan" = wheel spins up from rest at touchdown (TPG quote).
