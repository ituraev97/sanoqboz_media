# 048 — Mashina oynasidagi qora nuqtalar nima uchun? (frit)
- Hackaday, "Tech in Plain Sight: Windshield Frit" (2024-01-18): frit = ceramic-based baked-on enamel on the windshield edges, silk-screened; inner part is the bonding point for the adhesive; outer part blocks UV from reaching the adhesive; hides adhesive/wiring; black enamel heats up, dots create an even temperature gradient instead of a sharp transition (otherwise optical distortion, at worst cracking); frit behind the rearview mirror helps block sunlight: https://hackaday.com/2024/01/18/tech-in-plain-sight-windshield-frit
- Motor1, "Why Cars Have Tiny Black Dots Around The Windshield": ceramic enamel permanently fused to the windshield, not paint that peels; primary job — shield the adhesive from UV; frit absorbs more heat than clear glass; "lensing" (optical distortion) can occur where the black border meets clear glass; dots get progressively smaller: https://www.motor1.com/features/803150/why-car-windshields-have-black-dots/
- Reader's Digest (expert: Glass Doctor, 2025): enamel-based coating "frit" baked onto the inside surface; textured surface for polyurethane adhesives ("the rough surface gives the glue something to grip"); shields the adhesive from UV; band heats faster, dots distribute temperature, minimize "lensing"; dots behind the mirror = "a third visor of sorts": https://www.rd.com/article/black-dots-on-windshields-car-windows/
- SlashGear: enamel baked into the glass edges; dots help bonding with polyurethane adhesives, protect sealant from UV, spread heat evenly and reduce "lensing": https://www.slashgear.com/1909533/little-black-dots-car-windows-windshield-purpose-explained-what-used-for/
- Visuals: cross-section, UV rays, heat glow, lens-distortion grid and the cloth are schematic (exaggerated). The joke (mother-in-law wiping the dots) follows from "baked/fused" — the enamel does not wipe off.
