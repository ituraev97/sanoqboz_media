- FAA Lessons Learned, de Havilland DH-106 Comet 1 (G-ALYV / G-ALYP / G-ALYY): inaugural flight 2 May 1952 (first pressurized civilian jet transport); breakups 10 Jan 1954 (Elba, BOAC 781) and 8 Apr 1954 (Naples, SAA 201); cause = fatigue cracking at the corners of the squarish windows, stress at corners much higher than calculated; first fracture at the ADF window on top of the fuselage; tank-test fuselage G-ALYU failed at a window corner after ≈3 060 pressurized flights in total; expected life 10 000; modern windows are rounded so stress flows around them more evenly; Comet 1XB changed windows to oval: https://www.faa.gov/lessons_learned/transport_airplane/accidents/G-ALYV
- Smithsonian Magazine, "Comet's Tale": 2 May 1952 first scheduled jet passenger service; cabin pumped with air on the climb and bled off on descent — every cycle stresses the structure; fracture started in the corner of a window atop the aircraft; metal fatigue: https://www.smithsonianmag.com/history/comets-tale-63573615/
- "≈3 000" = rounded from FAA's ≈3 060 pressurized flights of the test fuselage ("taxminan" in the script).
- 3D model: "de Havilland DH 106 Comet 4" by helijah (Sketchfab, CC BY 4.0): https://sketchfab.com/3d-models/de-havilland-dh-106-comet-4-05ba73f3f2bb4050988e087f53e98dc9 — the Comet 4 already has the rounded windows; the square-window panel, stress map, crack, cabin "breathing" (exaggerated) and the split fuselage are schematic.
