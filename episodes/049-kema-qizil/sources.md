# 049 — Nega kemalarning tagi qizil? (antifouling bo‘yoq)
- Wikipedia, "Anti-fouling paint": copper paints historically red → ship bottoms still painted red today; mid-1700s thin copper sheets nailed onto hulls; paints with cuprous oxide/other biocides impede barnacles, algae; ablative paints slowly wear off releasing copper biocide: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-fouling_paint
- SlashGear, "This Is Why So Many Ships Are Painted Red On The Bottom" (2025): copper oxide compound in antifouling paint is typically red; acts as a biocide; copper sheathing effective in the 1700s (British Navy); fouling adds weight, drag, fuel; non-red antifouling paints exist; red seen as a historical nod: https://www.slashgear.com/1951574/why-ship-bottoms-painted-red/
- Mental Floss (2019): "The reason is tradition. And worms." Copper gave the paint a red tint; "These days, biocides can be mixed with virtually any color of paint": https://www.mentalfloss.com/article/596317/why-ships-are-often-red-on-bottom
- Schultz, Bendick, Holm, Hertel, "Economic impact of biofouling on a naval surface ship", Biofouling 27(1), 2011 (US Naval Academy): DDG-51 at 30 knots, heavy calcareous fouling → +47 % shaft power (+43 % resistance), Table 4: https://www.usna.edu/NAOE/_files/documents/Faculty/schultz/Schultz%2C%20Bendick%2C%20Holm%2C%20Hertel%20-%20Economic%20Impact%20of%20Biofouling%2C%202011.pdf
- "200+ yil": copper sheathing from the mid-1700s (Wikipedia, SlashGear) → over 250 years; we say "ikki yuz yildan ortiq" (safe lower bound).
- Visuals: barnacles, weed, copper plates, copper particles and larvae bouncing off are schematic. Joke (grandma's qozon) is a light comparison, not a claim.
